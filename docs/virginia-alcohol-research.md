# Virginia: alcohol and the TapIn Plus benefits

Research for Sam, 11 Sep 2026. Not legal advice: for counsel to confirm. Four sweeps (Virginia Administrative Code, Code of Virginia, Virginia ABC guidance, market practice), then every case re-checked against the primary text by an independent skeptic. Confidence is medium throughout.

> **Superseded in part, 14 Sep 2026 (Sam):** "credit can be used towards alcohol, it's just that 15% off can't be
> applied towards alcohol. Points can be earned on alcohol, redeemed for generic credit, then used towards
> alcohol purchases." The site now says so in one scope sentence on the pitch and the checkout. Section B's
> cautions below are NOT resolved by that decision and still need counsel: no credit on drinks 9 p.m.–2 a.m.,
> never two drinks for one credit, never a drink paid in full by credit without the one-per-24h gift rule,
> **never market the credit as earned by or meant for drinks (3VAC5-20-10 C 8)**, log the line it applied to,
> and never apply it for anyone who cannot lawfully be sold alcohol.

**Decisions (Sam, 11 Sep 2026).** All three benefits (the 15%, the $5 credit, and points, both earned and redeemed) apply only to food and non-alcoholic items, and drinks never count toward the $10. The savings estimate and the guarantee count only non-alcohol spend. Sam first let drinks earn points, then chose food-only points after the second check at the end of this document.

## Short answer

Not a blanket ban. Virginia bars happy-hour drink discounts from 9 p.m. to 2 a.m. (ABC: no discounts after 9 p.m.) and two-for-one deals anytime. A members-only 15% doesn't escape that; earned credits and points on drinks are unsettled. Safest: all three benefits food-only. Balances members prepaid, spent at full price, look fine. For counsel.

## A. Blanket 15% incl. drinks, all hours — Likely not allowed

A reduced drink price is likely a 'happy hour', barred 9 p.m.–2 a.m.; membership is no exemption. ABC guidance says no discounts after 9 p.m.; in 2004 the Board called a reduced price 'for a day or longer' still a happy hour. Before 9 p.m., a flat 15% is defensible.

**Limits**

- As specified (any hour), the exposed part is alcohol discounted from 9 p.m. to 2 a.m. The fix is to turn the 15% off alcohol lines at 9 p.m. The rules don't say whether order, payment or service time counts, so check both order time and fulfilment time. For counsel.
- The only exemption (3VAC5-50-160 D) is for prearranged private events, not open to the public, in a room used only for such events. Paying members don't fit it.
- At every hour, keep it a flat percentage: no bigger pour at the same price, no two-for-one, no unlimited drinks for one price (3VAC5-50-160 C 2–C 4).
- Venues must not reprice menus so the member price becomes 'customary' as a way past 9 p.m. (§ 4.1-324 A 13; § 4.1-325 A 23; 3VAC5-50-160 C 5).
- Never make the 15% or any perk depend on ordering alcohol (3VAC5-20-10 C 8).
- If ABC treats the discount as a retailer 'coupon', 3VAC5-20-90 B 4 allows those only on wine and beer sold for off-premises consumption. 'Coupon' is undefined.
- Record every discounted alcohol line. If records are incomplete, ABC may presume every drink sold at the highest posted menu price (3VAC5-70-90 E).
- The risk falls first on the venue: a first 'illegal happy hour' offense means a 10-day suspension or a $1,000 civil charge (3VAC5-70-210). TapIn's exposure comes through agency (§§ 4.1-202, 4.1-324, 4.1-325) and possibly § 4.1-323. For counsel.
- Re-check before the Spring 2027 launch. On Feb. 6, 2026 the Board took no action on moving the cutoff to midnight (Petition 444).

**Sources**

- [3VAC5-50-160 C 1. Happy hour and related promotions](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title3/agency5/chapter50/section160/): “No retail licensee shall engage in any of the following practices: 1. Conducting a happy hour between 9 p.m. of each day and 2 a.m. of the following day;”
- [Virginia ABC, Alcoholic Beverage Delivery Laws and Regulations FAQ (answer on delivery orders; read in browser 2026-09-11, WebFetch 403)](https://www.abc.virginia.gov/licenses/retail-resources/delivery-regulations): “Yes, happy hour pricing applies to delivery orders. No discounts are permitted after 9 p.m.”
- [ABC Board, Agency Background Document, 3 VAC 5-50 periodic review, Aug. 13, 2004 (action withdrawn 2006; non-binding)](https://townhall.virginia.gov/l/GetFile.cfm?File=2%5C1603%5C2892%5CAgencyStatement_ABC_2892_v1.pdf): “Temporarily reduced price, whether for a day or longer, will continue to be considered a happy hour.”

**Where the reviewers split.** Both A lenses rated this 'unclear' on the regulation text alone. They got HTTP 403 from ABC's pages and said that guidance would likely settle the question. The C lens read those pages in a browser, and this synthesis re-read them on 2026-09-11. Together with the 2004 Board statement, they lean toward 'no discounts after 9 p.m.', so the more cautious answer is taken. What cuts the other way: ABC guidance is not binding, the FAQ answer is about delivery orders, and the 2004 action was withdrawn. The same 2004 document also said a 'mug club' as described did not appear to be a happy hour, without describing it. The lenses also split on whether aiding-and-abetting (§ 4.1-323) reaches a breach of a regulation alone. The coupon risk was raised only by the applied lens.

## B. Earned $5 credit spent on drinks — Unsettled

No text found covers earned credits on drinks outside casinos (§ 4.1-206.3 A 14). A credit lowering a drink's price may be happy-hour pricing (no discounts after 9 p.m.), a retailer coupon (off-premises wine and beer only), or, if it makes a drink free, a restricted gift. Unsettled.

**Limits**

- Safest build: the $5 credit applies only to food and non-alcoholic lines and never lowers an alcohol line. That matches DoorDash, Grubhub+ and Instacart+ practice.
- If counsel later lets it touch alcohol, three rules hold: never between 9 p.m. and 2 a.m., never two or more drinks for one credit (3VAC5-50-160 C 3), and never a drink brought to $0.
- A drink the credit pays for in full risks being treated as a gift. The only gift exception that fits is one drink per patron with no repeat within 24 hours, taxed and recorded as a sale (3VAC5-70-100 B 3 and D).
- Earning on any $10 order is not literally conditioned on buying alcohol. Never market the credit as earned by drinks or meant for them (3VAC5-20-10 C 8).
- Do not raise in-app drink prices to fund credits (§ 4.1-324 A 13). Log where each credit was applied, line by line (3VAC5-70-90 E).
- The credit is not an age check: never apply it to alcohol for anyone who cannot lawfully be sold it (3VAC5-20-90 B 7).

**Sources**

- [3VAC5-70-100 A. Gifts of alcoholic beverages generally](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title3/agency5/chapter70/section100/): “Gifts of alcoholic beverages by a licensee to any other person are prohibited except as otherwise provided in this section”
- [Code of Virginia § 4.1-206.3 A 14 (mixed beverage casino licenses only)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title4.1/chapter2/section4.1-206.3/): “enable patrons who participate in a loyalty or reward credit program to redeem credits for the purchase of alcoholic beverages for on-premises consumption”
- [3VAC5-20-90 B 4. Advertising; coupons](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title3/agency5/chapter20/section90/): “Retail licensees may offer coupons, including their own discount or refund coupons, on wine and beer sold for off-premises consumption only.”

**Where the reviewers split.** Both lenses say unclear but weigh the risk differently. The literal lens finds the gift theory weak: an earned credit is arguably not 'gratuitous' under § 4.1-100. It also doubts a one-order credit is 'a specified period of time'. The applied lens relies on the Board's 2004 statement that a reduced price 'for a day or longer' is still a happy hour. It treats any credit that lowers a drink's price as happy-hour pricing: defensible before 9 p.m., exposed after. The cautious answer is taken: keep the credit off alcohol.

## B2. Member-prepaid balance, full price — Allowed, with limits

Paying full price with the member's own money is a sale, not a discount or gift, so on their text the happy-hour, coupon and gift rules don't reach it. The real issues sit elsewhere: TapIn's role in the sale, and money-transmission and gift-certificate rules for a shared balance.

**Limits**

- Alcohol lines always go at the venue's normal price. A load bonus ('pay $45, get $50'), or any member price on drinks, turns it back into Case A or B.
- Keep money members paid in on a ledger separate from earned credits and points, and let only paid-in money touch alcohol. Only casinos have express permission to accept reward credits for alcohol (§ 4.1-206.3 A 14).
- Do not advertise anything of value for spending the balance on drinks (3VAC5-20-10 C 8).
- 'Sale' includes receiving an order (§ 4.1-100), and unlicensed selling is a Class 1 misdemeanor (§ 4.1-302). TapIn should take orders and apply balances only as each venue's agent under a written contract (§ 4.1-202).
- A TapIn-issued balance spendable at five unrelated venues is not 'closed loop stored value' (§ 6.2-1922). Prefer per-venue balances, with TapIn collecting as agent under § 6.2-1923: a written agreement, the venue telling the public TapIn accepts its payments, and payment counted as received once TapIn has it. For counsel.
- A balance bought for money is likely a 'gift certificate': no dormancy, inactivity or service fees, and expiry must be disclosed (§§ 59.1-530, 59.1-531).
- Any percentage fee TapIn takes from venue income must fit a 3VAC5-50-220 exception.
- Delivery only: HB 688's official summary says third-party delivery licences were repealed effective July 1, 2026, yet the live Code still prints § 4.1-212.2. Pickup and dine-in do not trigger it.
- A funded balance is not an age check: no sales to people under 21 or intoxicated patrons.

**Sources**

- [Code of Virginia § 4.1-100 ("Sale" and "sell")](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title4.1/chapter1/section4.1-100/): “"Sale" and "sell" includes soliciting or receiving an order for; keeping, offering or exposing for sale; peddling, exchanging, or bartering; or delivering otherwise than gratuitously”
- [Code of Virginia § 4.1-202](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title4.1/chapter2/section4.1-202/): “The privilege of any licensee to sell or serve alcoholic beverages shall extend to such licensee and to all agents or employees of such licensee”
- [Code of Virginia § 6.2-1922 ("Closed loop stored value")](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title6.2/chapter19.1/section6.2-1922/): “"Closed loop stored value" means stored value that is redeemable by the issuer only for goods or services provided by the issuer or its affiliate”

**Where the reviewers split.** Only one lens reviewed B2, so there is no split on the answer. It corrected three research leads. 3VAC5 does use the word 'discount'. 3VAC5-50-220 does regulate percentage fees. And the official HB 688 summary contradicts the 'likely sunset only' reading of 2024 c. 105. The finding is an absence of a ban, not an express permission.

## C. 2x points on drinks, redeeming on drinks — Unsettled

Earning points on a full-price drink isn't a price cut or a gift, but advertising points tied to drinks falls within 3VAC5-20-10 C 8. Redeeming points on alcohol has express permission only for casinos; after 9 p.m. it's exposed, and multi-drink rewards are barred at any hour.

**Limits**

- Safest: earn no points on alcohol lines, and never let points, or rewards funded by points, be redeemed on alcohol. Outback, Buffalo Wild Wings, Starbucks and New Realm Brewing exclude alcohol.
- No '2x points on drinks' copy and no earn badges on alcohol items (3VAC5-20-10 C 8). No contest or prize draw that requires buying alcohol (C 7).
- If redemption on alcohol is ever allowed: never from 9 p.m. to 2 a.m., no 'two beers for 500 points' (3VAC5-50-160 C 3), and no free upsizes (C 2).
- A drink paid for entirely with points, if treated as a gift: at most one per patron per 24 hours, taxed and recorded as a sale (3VAC5-70-100 B 3 and D; § 4.1-111 B 16).
- No bonus-point nudges toward another round (3VAC5-50-80).
- Keep line-level records of every reduction on an alcohol line (3VAC5-70-90 E).

**Sources**

- [3VAC5-20-10 C 8. Advertising restrictions](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title3/agency5/chapter20/section10/): “Constitutes or contains an offer to pay or provide anything of value conditioned on the purchase of alcoholic beverages”
- [3VAC5-50-160 C 3](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title3/agency5/chapter50/section160/): “Selling two or more drinks for one price, such as "two for one" or "three for one"”
- [Code of Virginia § 4.1-111 B 16 (one-drink gift authority)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title4.1/chapter1/section4.1-111/): “only one such gift is given during any 24-hour period and subject to any Board limitations on the frequency of such gifts”

**Where the reviewers split.** Only one lens reviewed C. It rejected a research lead that the one-drink gift exception 'allows' free-drink rewards: 'gift' is undefined, and a drink earned through paid orders is arguably a sale at a reduced price, which brings in the 9 p.m. limit. It also found that fas REWARDS' state lists show nothing about Virginia. This was the only lens that read ABC's guidance pages, which the other lenses got HTTP 403 on.

## D. Food-only benefits — Allowed, with limits

Virginia's pricing and gift rules turn on the price of the alcohol itself. If every drink stays at menu price and benefits land only on food, happy-hour, gift and coupon rules aren't triggered on their text. Still open: alcohol counting toward the $10 that earns the credit.

**Limits**

- Apply the 15%, the credit and points line by line to non-alcohol items only. Never spread an order-level discount onto drinks, in TapIn's data or the venue POS.
- Count only non-alcohol spend toward the $10. The text does not say whether letting drinks count makes the credit an offer 'conditioned on' buying alcohol. At minimum, never tell members that drinks count.
- State the exclusion, for example '15% off food and non-alcoholic drinks'. 'Save 15% on every order' at a resto-bar risks being misleading by omission (3VAC5-20-10 C 4).
- Food ratio at The Burg: food-only discounts cut food receipts while spirits receipts stay the same. If monthly food sales are under $48,000, a 30% or 45% minimum still applies (§ 4.1-111 B 25). The $4,000 monthly food minimum also stands (3VAC5-50-110 C 2).
- Food paid for entirely by credit may not count as food receipts at all ('no sale', 3VAC5-70-90 E). For counsel.
- Record drinks at their regular price. Incomplete records let ABC presume every drink sold at the highest posted menu price (3VAC5-70-90 E).

**Sources**

- [3VAC5-50-160 A 1 (definition of happy hour)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title3/agency5/chapter50/section160/): “"Happy hour" means a specified period of time during which alcoholic beverages are sold at prices reduced from the customary price established by a retail licensee.”
- [3VAC5-20-10 C 8. Advertising restrictions](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title3/agency5/chapter20/section10/): “Constitutes or contains an offer to pay or provide anything of value conditioned on the purchase of alcoholic beverages”
- [Code of Virginia § 4.1-111 B 25 c (2026, c. 719)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title4.1/chapter1/section4.1-111/): “For such licensees with monthly food sales averaging at least $4,000, but less than $25,000, the food-to-beverage ratio shall meet or exceed 45 percent”

**Where the reviewers split.** Only one lens reviewed D. Research leads said C 8 'prohibits' letting alcohol count toward the $10. The skeptic called that overstated and rated it unclear, because any $10 order qualifies. The skeptic also rejected applying the gift rule and the anti-evasion clauses to a food-only design. The cautious answer is taken: exclude alcohol from the $10.

## E. Advertising member drink prices — Allowed, with limits

Advertising regular drink prices is allowed, and § 4.1-320 binds TapIn directly. A member drink price is a reduced price, so advertising it with no hour limit promotes a discount ABC says can't run 9 p.m.–2 a.m. Student-only channels add under-21 advertising risk.

**Limits**

- Under the food-only build, show no member prices on alcohol anywhere: not in the app, the deck or social posts. 'Draft beer $7.00, member price $5.95' with no hour limit is the exposed version.
- 'Members save 15% on every order' at The Burg must say alcohol is excluded (3VAC5-20-10 C 4).
- Keep drink prices and alcohol promotion out of student-only app screens, emails and social accounts. Whether those count as publications aimed mainly at people under 21 (3VAC5-20-40 A 2) is unsettled.
- Nothing that would tend to induce under-21 or excessive drinking (3VAC5-20-10 C 1). Age-gate alcohol items.
- Alcohol ads in electronic media, which includes the Internet, must name the responsible advertiser (3VAC5-20-40 A 1 and B).
- Pitch decks are the least clearly 'advertising'. Label illustrative drink prices as hypothetical, and do not post decks publicly or send them to students.
- Penalties: violating § 4.1-320 is a Class 1 misdemeanor. For a venue's first offense, 'Illegally advertising happy hour' and 'Unauthorized advertising' each carry a 10-day suspension or a $1,000 civil charge (3VAC5-70-210).

**Sources**

- [Code of Virginia § 4.1-320 A. Illegal advertising](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title4.1/chapter3/section4.1-320/): “Except in accordance with this subtitle and Board regulations, no person shall advertise in or send any advertising matter into the Commonwealth about or concerning alcoholic beverages”
- [3VAC5-20-40 A 2. Advertising; print and electronic media](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title3/agency5/chapter20/section40/): “Advertisements of alcoholic beverages are prohibited in publications not of general circulation that are distributed or intended to be distributed primarily to persons under 21 years of age.”
- [Virginia ABC, Advertising (Retail Resources; read in browser 2026-09-11, WebFetch 403)](https://www.abc.virginia.gov/licenses/retail-resources/advertising): “Happy hour/drink specials may not be offered between 9 p.m. and 2 a.m.”

**Where the reviewers split.** Only one lens reviewed E. Research leads said C 8 'prohibits' 'members save 15% on every order'. The skeptic found that a weak fit, because the saving is not conditioned on buying alcohol. It placed the real exposure in the 9 p.m. rule reached through § 4.1-320, misleading omission (C 4), and the under-21 publication rule. That skeptic could not fetch ABC's Advertising page; its 9 p.m. line was later confirmed in a browser.

## What to build

- Tag every menu item alcohol or non-alcohol from the venue POS; the 15%, the $5 credit and points redemptions apply only to non-alcohol lines.
- Never spread order-level discounts or credits onto alcohol lines; log drinks at menu price and where each discount, credit and point landed.
- Count only non-alcohol spend toward the $10 that earns the weekly credit, and earn no points on alcohol lines.
- Member-prepaid balances may pay full price for drinks: separate ledger from earned credits, no load bonuses, issued per venue with TapIn as agent.
- Any future alcohol perk: off from 9 p.m. to close, no multi-drink or free-upsize rewards, only after written ABC special-agent confirmation.
- Copy says '15% off food and non-alcoholic drinks'; no member drink prices or drink nudges in student marketing; age-gate alcohol items.

## The estimate

Savings and venue-cost estimates should exclude alcohol spend: model the 15%, credit eligibility and points on non-alcohol subtotals only. The Burg's member value drops most. Add build scope for item alcohol flags, line-level allocation and separate balance ledgers.

## Second check: earning points on drinks, redeeming on food only

Run 11 Sep 2026 after Sam chose to let drinks earn points. Two reviewers, each trying to refute the design, then a cautious synthesis. Research for counsel to confirm, not legal advice.

**Result: Likely not allowed (low confidence).** Points earned on drinks are likely prohibited (for counsel to confirm). Leave alcohol out of earning, like the discount.

A Virginia rule bars advertising 'anything of value conditioned on the purchase of alcoholic beverages', and arguably reaches TapIn, not just the venue. Points returning 10% to 20% of drink spend plausibly fit. Neither exception applies: happy hour combinations, or coupons, which bars can use only on take-home wine and beer. ABC reportedly stopped per-beer charity donations in 2024, suggesting the problem is value that grows with drink sales, not the wording. No ABC ruling was found.

**How the two reviewers split**

- **Text: Unsettled (medium).** Strongest argument against allowing it: The case against "allowed": 3VAC5-20-10 C 8 bars advertising that "contains an offer to pay or provide anything of value conditioned on the purchase of alcoholic beverages". Under § 4.1-320 A, that rule reaches any "person", so it reaches TapIn directly, not just the venue. Members get points for every dollar spent on drinks: roughly 10 cents per drink dollar at 10 points and about 1 cent each, or about 20 cents if 10 is the rate before doubling. That can be more than the 15% discount the founder already kept off alcohol. It runs at every hour, 9 p.m. to 2 a.m. included, and only members get it. Take a drink off the order and the points go down. Showing only the order total changes how visible this is, not what is offered. The program terms have to say that points build on the whole order, drinks included, so any in-app or marketing statement of those terms contains the offer. Neither C 8 exception fits. Exception (i) covers only a discounted food-and-drink combination during permitted happy hour hours, and points are neither a combination price nor limited by hour. Exception (ii) covers "refund coupons". But 3VAC5-20-90 B 4 lets retailers offer their own coupons only on "wine and beer sold for off-premises consumption only", so a resto-bar cannot use it for drinks served on site. The rules as a whole allow value tied to alcohol only through narrow, listed channels: happy hour combinations by the hour, retailer coupons for take-home wine and beer, and loyalty credits written into statute for casinos alone (§ 4.1-206.3 A 14). Nothing in the text allows loyalty value earned on drinks at an ordinary on-premises licensee. The best reply against calling it prohibited: "conditioned on" naturally means alcohol must be bought to get the value. C 7 uses "required" in the same sense. Here points come from any spending and no drink is ever required. A points total on a mixed order names no drink, so arguably it is not "alcoholic beverage advertising". And 3VAC5-20-10 A makes permission the default.

- **Practice: Likely not allowed (low).** Strongest argument against allowing it: 3VAC5-20-10 C 8 only reaches an offer "conditioned on the purchase of alcoholic beverages." TapIn Plus points accrue on the order total whether or not alcohol is bought. A food-only order earns at the same rate, and the app never shows value for buying a drink, so arguably no offer is conditioned on alcohol.

The Helene fundraiser differs in three ways. It was an advertised promotion on one beer, paying a fixed amount per unit. The account is a newspaper report with a second-hand statement from an ABC agent. It is not a Final Decision.

No Virginia statute, regulation or ABC decision found says anything about earning loyalty points. The casino statute covers only redemption, which TapIn already excludes. The happy hour definition and the gift rule, by their text, do not reach sales at full price.

National operators with Virginia locations publish terms that earn on alcohol, and Virginia is not among the states they exclude:
- World of Beer (Richmond open)
- Dave & Buster's (Virginia Beach, Springfield, Glen Allen, per search results)
- Bar Louie (Ashburn, Woodbridge, per search results)

Bar Louie's model mirrors TapIn's: it earns on alcohol when the check includes food, and its rewards apply only to food. Points also resemble card cash back on a bar tab, which nobody treats as a price cut.

**If drinks were to earn anyway, the conditions**

- For counsel to confirm: calculate points only on food and non-alcoholic dollars; alcohol never adds points.
- Never let points pay for alcohol, directly or through cash, stored credit or a bar tab.
- Show points against the qualifying amount, not the order total; no copy anywhere ties points to drinks.
- Age-gate alcohol items and keep drink promotion out of channels aimed mainly at under-21 students.
- No alcohol maker, importer or wholesaler funds or co-brands benefits; keep the no-nudge rule.
- If drinks keep earning, the venue gets written ABC guidance or a variance before launch.

**Notes.** The two checks disagree.

- **Text check: unclear.** "Conditioned on" can mean a drink must be bought, and points on the whole order require no drink. 3VAC5-20-10 A also makes advertising permitted unless a rule says otherwise.
- **Practice check: likely prohibited.** In 2024, ABC reportedly barred value tied to how much beer was sold. Most chains found leave alcohol out of earning. Bar Louie, Dave & Buster's and World of Beer do earn on alcohol, and their terms do not exclude Virginia, but whether their Virginia locations actually do so was not verified.

This answer takes the more cautious view. Neither check found anything that permits earning on drinks. Both agree that earning only on food and non-alcoholic items is reached by no rule they read.
